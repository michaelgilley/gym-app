# Program design research notes

**Date**: 2026-05-14
**Context**: Validating whether to keep Day E (Wednesday arms + carries) given the conflict with Day C (Thursday pull). Goal priority: hypertrophy first, recovery/energy second. Peer-reviewed literature only.

---

## TL;DR verdict

**Keep Day E. Move it to Friday.** The case rests on **weekly volume**, not training frequency. Move resolves the 24-hour same-muscle recovery conflict that compromises Thursday pull-day performance.

---

## Honest correction to earlier reasoning

Initial claim was "2x/wk frequency beats 1x/wk by ~50% growth." This was based on **Schoenfeld, Ogborn, Krieger 2016** (Sports Med, PMID 27102172), which is now superseded.

* **Schoenfeld, Grgic, Krieger 2019** (J Sports Sci 37(11):1286-1295, DOI 10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906, PMID 30558493): the same group re-ran the meta with volume properly equated. **Frequency effect disappeared.** Their conclusion: "for a given training volume, individuals can choose a weekly frequency per muscle group based on personal preference."
* **Pelland, Remmert, Robinson, Hinson, Zourdos 2026** (Sports Med 56(2):481-505, PMID 41343037, Bayesian meta-regression, 67 studies, n=2,058): posterior probability that frequency's marginal slope on hypertrophy exceeds zero was "compatible with negligible effects"; volume's was 100%.
* **Grgic et al. 2018** (J Sci Med Sport, DOI 10.1016/j.jsams.2018.01.002): narrative review reaches the same conclusion.

So the argument for Day E must be made on **volume**, not frequency.

## Volume dose-response (the actual lever)

* **Schoenfeld, Ogborn, Krieger 2017** (J Sports Sci 35(11):1073-1082, PMID 27433992): each additional weekly set increases hypertrophy ES by 0.023 (p=0.002, ~0.37%/set). Higher-vs-lower volume contrast ES 0.241 (p=0.03, ~3.9% greater gain). Most underlying studies capped at <10 direct sets/wk, so upper bound was not characterized.
* **Baz-Valle et al. 2022** (J Hum Kinet 81:199-210, PMID 35291645): trained men, >=1yr experience. Recommended **12-20 weekly sets/muscle**. Per-muscle: biceps no significant moderate-vs-high diff (p=0.59); **triceps significantly favored >20 sets/wk** (p=0.01); quads p=0.19.
* **Pelland et al. 2026** (PMID 41343037): probability volume-hypertrophy slope exceeds zero = 100%; diminishing returns present but **far less pronounced for hypertrophy than for strength**. Weekly point-of-undetectable-superiority ~31 fractional sets. Per-session PUOS ~11 fractional sets (Remmert et al. SportRxiv, DOI 10.51224/SRXIV.537).

**Fractional-set convention**: direct sets count at 1.0, indirect (compound that hits muscle as synergist) at 0.5, distant at 0.

### Applied to my program

| State | Biceps direct | Biceps indirect (frac) | Biceps total frac sets/wk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Without Day E | ~3 (pull-day curl) | ~4 (rows+pulldowns at 0.5) | **~7** (below Baz-Valle 12-20 range) |
| With Day E | ~13 (3 pull + 10 arm) | ~4 | **~17** (in productive zone) |

Triceps similar. Triceps in particular have direct meta-analytic support (Baz-Valle 2022) for >20 sets/wk.

## Direct vs indirect isolation in trained lifters

Mixed evidence. Weakest link in the case.

* **Gentil et al. 2013** (Asian J Sports Med, PMID 24149055, untrained men, 10 wk): MJ-only +6.10% elbow-flexor thickness vs SJ-only +5.83%. No significant between-group diff.
* **de França et al. 2015** (Appl Physiol Nutr Metab 40(8):822-826, PMID 26244600, DOI 10.1139/apnm-2015-0109, **trained men >=2yr**, 8 wk): MJ-only vs MJ+SJ. **No significant added growth from isolation.** But underpowered (n=20), short, used tape circumference (crude).
* **Mannarino et al. 2021** (J Strength Cond Res 35(10):2677-2681, PMID 31268995, untrained, within-subject): SJ curl produced **+11.06% elbow-flexor thickness vs +5.16% for unilateral DB row** (~2x advantage to isolation).
* **Brandao et al. 2020** (J Strength Cond Res, PMID 32149887): MJ+SJ produced greater regional triceps CSA than MJ alone in some heads.

### Credibility flag

* **Barbalho et al. 2018** ("Influence of Adding Single-Joint Exercise...") was **retracted in 2021** (PMID 34941614) after Vigotsky et al. flagged improbable data patterns. Multiple Barbalho papers retracted. Do not cite his body of work.

### Net

Expect added arm growth from isolation, especially for triceps. Don't expect dramatic. Trained-lifter evidence base is thin and partially contaminated by retracted work.

## Inter-session recovery (the Wed -> Thu problem)

* **Soares et al. 2015** (J Strength Cond Res, PMID 25807025, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000000945, resistance-trained men): after 8x10RM preacher curls, elbow-flexor peak torque was still **~8.4% below baseline at 24 hours**; DOMS elevated through 72 hr. Same study: seated row's elbow-flexor PT recovered within 24 hr.
* **Ferreira et al. 2021** (Sport Sci Health, PMID 33394892, DOI 10.1007/s11332-021-00761-8): replicated; PT/DOMS/CK depressed 24-48 hr post single-joint elbow flexor work.
* **Damas et al. 2016** (J Physiol, PMID 27219125, DOI 10.1113/JP272472): force decrement largest early in a block, attenuates with repeated-bout effect, but does not fully normalize within 24 hr after novel high-volume bouts.
* **Paulsen et al. 2012** (Exerc Immunol Rev 18:42-97, PMID 22876722): "mild" damage typically resolves within a few days; high-volume eccentric-biased can extend >1 wk.
* **Williams et al. 2014** (BioMed Res Int, PMID 24804232, DOI 10.1155/2014/329328): prior biceps work reduced subsequent peak power and grip strength.
* **Schoenfeld 2016 frequency review** and **Grgic 2018**: practical minimum **48 hr** between same-muscle sessions at matched volume.

### Practical implication

24 hr between high-volume direct curls and Thursday pull = ~5-10% load decrement on Thursday curls and modest decrement on grip-dependent rows. Lat-driven pulling mostly spared. 48+ hr fixes this.

---

## Evidence quality summary

| Claim | Quality | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Volume drives hypertrophy across 10-30 weekly sets | **Strong** | Multiple recent meta-analyses converging |
| Frequency does not add growth at equated volume | **Moderate-Strong** | Three independent meta-analyses agree |
| Elbow flexors need >24 hr recovery after high-volume isolation | **Strong** | Direct controlled studies in trained men |
| Adding isolation in *trained* lifters drives extra arm growth | **Weak-Moderate** | One direct underpowered trial (de França 2015) shows no effect; supporting evidence is indirect or in untrained subjects; retracted Barbalho work removes prior support |

## Recommended split

| Day | Workout |
|---|---|
| Mon | Day A: Push |
| Tue | Day B: Quad |
| Wed | Day C: Pull |
| Thu | Day D: Hinge |
| Fri | Day E: Arms + Carries |
| Sat | Rest |
| Sun | Rest |

* Biceps gap: Wed pull -> Fri arms = 48 hr. Within recovery window.
* Triceps gap: Mon push -> Fri arms = 96 hr. Well-recovered.
* Quad/Hinge gap: Tue -> Thu = 48 hr. Within window.
* Weekend full recovery before Mon push.
